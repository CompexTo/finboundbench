# ICAIF 2026 submission rulebook

This checklist is based on the official ICAIF 2026 Call for Papers and current ACM authorship guidance. Re-verify before submission because conference instructions can change.

## Mandatory conference rules

- Submit through Microsoft CMT.
- Deadline: 2 August 2026, 23:59 Anywhere on Earth (UTC-12).
- Maximum eight pages total in ACM `sigconf` two-column format, including figures and references.
- PDF only.
- The submission must be self-contained.
- No supplementary appendix or supplementary files.
- Use the latest ACM article template with `sigconf` and `anonymous` for review.
- Double-blind: remove author names, affiliations, acknowledgements and identifying repository links.
- Self-cite in the third person; do not say “our previous work.”
- No rebuttal period.
- Author list and order are final at initial CMT submission.
- Each individual author may appear on no more than six ICAIF submissions.
- Authors may be asked to perform a light reviewing load.
- Do not submit work that is already published, accepted, or under review at another archival venue.
- Do not submit to another archival venue during ICAIF review.
- A technical report or arXiv preprint can be permissible, but anonymity must be preserved and the preprint must not be cited in the blind submission.
- ORCID is required for all authors of accepted papers at camera-ready stage.
- At least one author should be ready to present physically in Milan; no prerecorded presentation is accepted.

## ACM authorship and AI-assisted work

- Only humans satisfying ACM authorship criteria can be authors.
- Authors remain responsible for code, data, claims, citations and generated material.
- Use of AI as part of the research method must be documented sufficiently for reproducibility.
- Current ACM policy no longer automatically requires disclosure for AI used only to assist writing, but conference-specific rules may be stricter. For this project, maintain a complete internal AI-use log and include a conservative disclosure if ICAIF or the final submission form requests it.

## Suggested eight-page allocation

This allocation is a planning recommendation, not an ICAIF rule.

1. Abstract — 0.2 page
2. Introduction and contributions — 0.8 page
3. Related work — 0.8 page
4. Problem definition and threat model — 0.8 page
5. Benchmark and method — 1.5 pages
6. Compex/PBER architecture — 1.0 page
7. Experimental setup — 0.8 page
8. Results and ablations — 1.5 pages
9. Limitations, ethics and conclusion — 0.6 page

Keep references compact. Every figure must carry experimental information; avoid product diagrams that do not support a claim.
